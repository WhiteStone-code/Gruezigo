# GrüeziGo vs. una app de idiomas — lesson structure comparison and restructuring plan

Research done via web search/fetch (no app install available). Sources are cited inline; full list at the bottom.

## 1. How a una app de idiomas lesson actually works

**Unit vs. lesson.** una app de idiomas's course is organized as CEFR-level → **unit/chapter** → **lesson**, shown to the learner as a horizontal/vertical **timeline path** so they always see "where I am and what's next" ([screensdesign.com](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/una app de idiomas-language-learning)). A unit groups 3-6 lessons around one communicative goal ("introducing yourself," "ordering food"); a lesson itself is further split into **3-4 "mini-lessons"** on the *same* small vocabulary set, not one long undifferentiated screen list ([una app de idiomas.com/methodology](https://www.una app de idiomas.com/en/it-works/una app de idiomas-methodology), [mezzoguild.com](https://www.mezzoguild.com/una app de idiomas-review/)). Between units there's an optional **Checkpoint quiz** (score ≥80% to skip ahead) rather than a mandatory linear grind ([testprepinsight.com](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/una app de idiomas-review/)).

**Anatomy of one lesson, in order** (synthesized across sources — [FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/reviews/una app de idiomas/), [alllanguageresources.com](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/una app de idiomas/), [mezzoguild.com](https://www.mezzoguild.com/una app de idiomas-review/), [studyfrenchspanish.com](https://www.studyfrenchspanish.com/una app de idiomas-review/)):

1. **Vocabulary intro** — a short set of words/phrases (typically 6-10) shown as flashcards: target text + native-speaker audio + image, sometimes short video of a real person using the phrase in context.
2. **Contextual dialogue** — the same words appear inside a native-speaker dialogue/video; learner fills gaps or answers comprehension questions ("Is Luis describing how he feels?" rather than "what does this mean?") — meaning-first, not raw translation.
3. **Controlled practice, mixed formats, one after another but each short** — matching pairs, fill-in-the-blank, true/false, sentence/word-order puzzles ("unscramble"), all drawing on the *same* just-taught set. Crucially, formats **rotate every 1-2 items** rather than running the whole vocab list through one exercise type before switching.
4. **Grammar bite** (Premium) — a short explanit rule tied directly to what was just practiced, not front-loaded before vocabulary.
5. **Free production** — a writing prompt or a speaking-repetition prompt, later sent to the community/AI for correction.
6. **End-of-lesson check** — a short ungraded quiz ("no grade, so don't panic") OR the writing exercise itself serves as the closer; no separate high-stakes final exam per lesson.

**Timing.** Consistently reported as **5-10 minutes per lesson** (some sources say "5 min average," others "5-15 min" depending on level) — short enough to do standing on a bus ([FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/reviews/una app de idiomas/), [testprepinsight.com](https://testprepinsight.com/comparisons/una app de idiomas-vs-otra app de idiomas/)). The onboarding **Study Plan** explicitly asks the user how many minutes/day they have and sizes lessons to fit — pacing is a designed input, not an emergent side-effect of content volume.

**Review system (separate from lessons).** A dedicated **Review/Smart Review** tab, distinct from the lesson flow, aggregates every word and grammar point ever taught and buckets them **Weak → Strong** using spaced repetition. Missed items resurface later **in a different exercise format** than they were first missed in, not an immediate identical retry ([help.una app de idiomas.com](https://help.una app de idiomas.com/hc/en-gb/articles/16941990776593-How-can-I-review-my-vocabulary), [mezzoguild.com](https://www.mezzoguild.com/una app de idiomas-review/)). Review is opt-in/on-demand, not force-inserted at the end of every single lesson.

**Streaks/social.** Streak is shown quietly on the profile page, not gamified into the lesson flow itself ([apps.apple.com listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/una app de idiomas-language-learning-app/id379968583)). The standout differentiator from otra app de idiomas is the **community correction system**: written/spoken free-production exercises get corrected by other native-speaking users (with points awarded for giving corrections), plus optional 1:1 messaging with community members ([screensdesign.com](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/una app de idiomas-language-learning), [una app de idiomas.com/methodology](https://www.una app de idiomas.com/en/it-works/una app de idiomas-methodology)).

**How it differs from otra app de idiomas in rhythm.** Both apps target similar overall session length, but reviewers consistently frame the *feel* differently: otra app de idiomas is bite-sized, gamified, grammar-light, algorithmic-only feedback; una app de idiomas is calmer, more "classroom methodology," leads with **meaning/context (video, dialogue) before drilling**, treats grammar as a first-class citizen woven into the lesson rather than a separate track, and closes the loop with **human feedback** instead of just a score ([testprepinsight.com](https://testprepinsight.com/comparisons/una app de idiomas-vs-otra app de idiomas/), [screensdesign.com](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/una app de idiomas-language-learning)). The other recurring critique, useful as a warning: even una app de idiomas's own reviewers say its Review tab and later grammar lessons can feel **repetitive/formulaic** once the format-rotation trick runs out of variety — worth noting so GrüeziGo doesn't over-rotate into its own staleness.

## 2. Where GrüeziGo's current structure diverges

Looked at `src/components/lesson/LessonView.jsx` and a real lesson file (`src/data/lessons/a1/a1-01-greetings.json`, itself a small lesson: 6 vocab words). Mapped point by point:

| una app de idiomas | GrüeziGo today | Divergence |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson = 3-4 short mini-lessons on the *same* small vocab set, each mixing intro + practice | One monolithic `steps` array: example → comparison table → match → listening → sentence → speaking → MC → recap → exam → complete | GrüeziGo has **no mini-lesson grouping at all** — it's one long corridor of ~9-10 full-screen steps end to end. There is no natural "you've finished chunk 1 of 3" breakpoint. |
| Exercise **format rotates every 1-2 vocab items** (match → gap-fill → true/false → …) | Each exercise component runs the **entire lesson's vocabulary through one format before switching**: `ExerciseMatchVocabulary` drills all 6 words, *then* `ExerciseListening` drills all 6 words again, *then* sentence builder, *then* speaking, *then* MC | This is the single biggest structural gap and matches the user's own diagnosis almost exactly. una app de idiomas interleaves formats at the item level; GrüeziGo interleaves at the *screen* level only, so the same 6 words get drilled 3-4 times back-to-back in near-identical ways before the lesson ever moves on. |
| Vocab intro happens via flashcard/video *before* any dialogue or drilling | GrüeziGo shows dialogue first, then a comparison table (8 rows — 2 more items than the vocab list itself), *then* vocabulary matching as the first real "learn" moment | Vocabulary is effectively introduced **twice, redundantly** (comparison table rows ≈ vocab list) before it's ever drilled once — no dedicated flashcard-style "meet this word" beat with audio-first framing. |
| One short ungraded quiz OR writing task closes the lesson | GrüeziGo closes with: MC exercise → auto-generated "recap" MC quiz (reusing the same vocab again) → 10-question final exam → completion screen | That's **three** quiz-shaped closers stacked in a row (MC, recap, exam), all still testing the same 6 words for the fourth/fifth/sixth time. una app de idiomas's single light check happens once, per mini-lesson, not three times at the tail of the whole lesson. |
| Free production (writing/speaking) is *one component of several*, feedback deferred to community | Speaking is a full dedicated step using live mic input; no writing/production exercise at all | Reasonable given no backend for community correction (see §4), but speaking currently sits as one more full-screen step in the corridor rather than a short beat woven between other formats. |
| Lesson takes 5-10 minutes total | For a 6-word lesson: 1 dialogue + 8-row table + 6-item match + 6-item listening + 2 sentence-builder + 2 speaking + 4 MC + 6-item recap + 10-question exam = **~45 discrete interactions** before completion | This is almost certainly the real source of the "3-5 min una app de idiomas vs. way longer GrüeziGo lesson" complaint — not any single step being slow, but the sheer number of sequential full-screen steps stacked for one small vocab set. |
| Checkpoint quiz is optional, gates unit progression, not every lesson | Every lesson ends in a mandatory 10-question final exam regardless of lesson size | Exam length is fixed at 10 regardless of how much content the lesson actually taught (6 words here) — disproportionate for a "chunk" this small; una app de idiomas-style checkpoints are unit-level and periodic, not per-lesson. |
| Review tab is separate from the lesson flow, spaced-repetition driven | GrüeziGo already has a separate "Repaso" hub (Grammar/Verbs/Vocabulary/Practice/Stats) — structurally the right idea | This part is actually *aligned* with una app de idiomas already; the problem is confined to the in-lesson flow, not the standalone review system. |

## 3. Concrete restructuring recommendations (ranked by impact)

**#1 — Interleave exercise formats per vocab chunk instead of running the whole word list through one format at a time.** *(Biggest impact, medium-size change.)*
Split `lesson.vocabulary` into chunks of 2-3 words. For each chunk, run one short "intro" beat (flashcard-style: word + audio, replacing/absorbing what the comparison table currently does for those words) immediately followed by **one** practice format on just that chunk, then move to the next chunk with a *different* format. Concretely: change `LessonView`'s `steps` builder from `list.push({type:'match'})` (whole-vocab) to something that maps over chunks, e.g. `vocabChunks.flatMap((chunk, i) => [{type:'intro', chunk}, {type: FORMAT_CYCLE[i % FORMAT_CYCLE.length], chunk}])` where `FORMAT_CYCLE = ['match','listening','sentence']`. This requires each exercise component (`ExerciseMatchVocabulary`, `ExerciseListening`, etc.) to accept a vocab *subset* instead of the full array — they mostly already take `vocabulary` as a prop, so this is a prop-shape tweak, not a rewrite. **No new data files needed**, just reshaping how `LessonView` slices existing `lesson.vocabulary`/`lesson.exercises` arrays into the step list.

**#2 — Collapse the triple-quiz ending into one.** *(High impact, small change.)*
Drop either the standalone `multipleChoice` step or the auto-generated `recap` step (they're redundant — both are MC quizzes over the same vocab) and shrink the final exam from a fixed 10 questions to something proportional to lesson size (e.g. `Math.min(6, vocabulary.length * 1.5)`). This alone removes ~15-20 interactions from every lesson. Pure logic change inside `LessonView.jsx` + `practicePool.js`'s `buildVocabQuizQuestions`; no data-shape change.

**#3 — Merge the comparison table into the vocabulary-intro beat instead of showing it as a separate full-screen step before anything is drilled.** *(Medium impact, small-to-medium change.)*
Right now `comparisonTable` (8 rows) and `vocabulary` (6 items) are two separate arrays covering almost the same content, shown as two consecutive full-reads before any practice. Fold the Hochdeutsch-vs-Schwiizerdütsch contrast into the per-chunk vocab-intro card from #1 (small "vs. Hochdeutsch: X" caption on the flashcard) rather than a standalone table screen. This is a data-shape decision: either derive `comparisonTable` rows from `vocabulary` entries directly (drop the separate array, add an optional `hochdeutsch` field already present per vocab item — it already exists per vocab entry) or keep `comparisonTable` only for grammar-pattern rows that have no 1:1 vocab item. Component-level change to `ComparisonTable.jsx`/new small `VocabIntroCard.jsx`.

**#4 — Introduce a "mini-lesson" grouping with its own tiny progress marker, mirroring una app de idiomas's 3-4-part structure.** *(Medium impact, medium change — new component.)*
Wrap groups of {intro-chunk + practice-chunk} into 2-3 visible "parts" with a short celebratory micro-break between them (a one-line "Part 1 of 3 done ✓" toast, not a full completion screen). This is what makes a lesson *feel* like una app de idiomas's contained mini-lessons instead of one long grind, even before touching total interaction count. Needs a new lightweight component (`LessonPartBreak.jsx`) and a `LessonProgressBar` update to show part boundaries, not just a continuous ratio.

**#5 — Make the recap/exam sizes and step count driven by `vocabulary.length`, not fixed.** *(Small change, but prevents regressions.)*
`XP_PER_LESSON`, exam length, and recap length are currently constants disconnected from lesson size. Tie them to `lesson.vocabulary.length` (e.g., exam = `~1.5x` vocab count, capped) so a future 10-word lesson doesn't silently regenerate the same bloat this analysis just found in a 6-word one. Small change in `LessonView.jsx` + `practicePool.js`.

**#6 — Speaking as a short embedded beat, not a dedicated full page, for early levels.** *(Small-medium impact, small change.)*
Keep the existing skip escape hatch, but for A1 lessons consider folding 1 speaking prompt into a chunk (per #1) rather than a standalone step with N prompts — matches una app de idiomas's "repeat after the native speaker" being a quick beat, not its own screen with multiple items.

Suggested rough new step count for a 6-word lesson after #1+#2+#3: dialogue (1) → [intro+practice] × 3 chunks (6) → 1 short check (1) → complete (1) = **~9 screens instead of ~10 heavier ones but each screen now covers only 2-3 words, not all 6**, and total quiz-shaped tail-end content drops from 3 stacked quizzes to 1. Combined with #4's part breaks, this should get real elapsed time much closer to una app de idiomas's 5-10 min band for small lessons.

## 4. What NOT to copy from una app de idiomas

- **Community/peer correction.** Requires a backend, moderation, and a live user base — none of which fits "free, local-only PWA." Skip entirely; GrüeziGo's speaking/writing checks should stay self-graded (speech recognition confidence, exact/fuzzy string match) rather than promise human feedback it can't deliver.
- **Checkpoint-gated unit progression tied to a server-tracked score threshold.** Fine to keep a *local* equivalent (don't unlock next level until X% average), but don't build anything resembling account-based gating, leaderboards, or synced progress — there's no backend to sync to.
- **Subscription-tiered content (Premium grammar lessons, una app de idiomas Live tutoring, translations into 15 languages).** GrüeziGo is free and local; don't replicate a paywall-shaped content split (e.g., "grammar explanations only for paying users"). All levels should get the same depth.
- **Video-of-a-real-person content.** una app de idiomas licenses native-speaker video; GrüeziGo has no production pipeline for this and it's not necessary — audio (already present via Web Speech API synthesis/`audioText`) plus the existing chat-bubble dialogue format covers the same "hear it in context" job at zero cost.
- **Streak-driven notification/reminder engine.** una app de idiomas's Study Plan sends push notifications tied to a chosen schedule. As a PWA without reliable background push on all platforms, don't over-invest here — the existing local activity-tracking calendar already covers the "did I practice" motivational loop without needing server-side scheduled notifications.
- **Points-for-corrections economy.** That whole incentive loop only exists to bootstrap the community-correction feature above; irrelevant without it.
